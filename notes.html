Notes · the open drawer

Three notes, set while still warm.

I write these the way I would explain them at a table. If a paragraph needs a priest, it is not finished.

If you cannot say it plainly, you do not have it

The first week I used a language model, I copied the way other people talked about it. Tokens. Context windows. Alignment. I sounded busy. I did not sound like I understood anything.

A composing room has a useful test. If the type is too small to read across the table, you have not set a page — you have set a secret. I started using the same test on my own notes. Could I read them to someone who does not live on this website? If not, back in the drawer.

Plain English is not dumbing down. It is the work. A model that writes in five registers is not the same thing as a person who can choose one. I am trying to become the second kind of person. The tools are the cabinet. The sentence is the print.

So the rule is simple, and I break it often: no word that I could not defend at dinner. No diagram where a paragraph would do. No claim that the software is a colleague. It is a very fast junior with no memory of last Tuesday and no reputation to lose. That is powerful. It is also why the human still has to sign the page.

Indian cities are the real interface problem

People ask me what I will “build with AI.” I keep coming back to the municipal corporation. Not because it is fashionable. Because it is the interface most of us actually touch, and it is broken in ways a chatbot will not fix by itself.

A city that spends a few percent of the public purse cannot pretend to be run. Wards have names. Categories of complaint have names. The people responsible have names too, though you would not know it from the portals. I have been indexing the obvious: which ward, which kind of problem, who is supposed to pick up the phone. It is unglamorous. It is also the sort of alphabet a country forgets to set.

The prompt bar is a pleasure. The BMC website is not. That gap is the job. If these tools are good for anything civic, they are good for making the boring machinery legible — not for generating another vision document. I would rather a resident know who to call about a flooded street than another paragraph about the future of urban intelligence.

Walk the city. Take the picture. Then ask who owns the drain in the frame. That is the whole method, so far.

Learning in public is a kind of typesetting

You do not wait for every sort in the case. You ink what you have and you print tonight. Some letters will be missing. The page will still be a page.

I am a beginner on purpose. I say so at the top of this site because the other option is to impersonate a lab. The impersonation is easy now. The models will help you do it. I am more interested in the record of someone catching up — Hyderabad evenings, a camera on the sill, a notebook that is allowed to be wrong on Monday and less wrong on Thursday.

Public learning has a cost. People will quote the unfinished sentence. Good. That is how type gets corrected. The alternative is a private drawer that never meets a reader, which is how a lot of “thought leadership” is born and why so little of it can be used.

This site is the cabinet I can keep. Twenty-six letters of a practice, not a manifesto. If you want the first drafts, they still go out on X. If you want the pictures, they live on Instagram. If you want the version I was willing to set in metal, it is here.
